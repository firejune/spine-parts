# sample

A generated full-body character — prompted as an adult woman with long wavy
hair in a cream blouse and a navy pinafore dress, standing with her hands
clasped in front of the waist — made to
be this repository's plain public fixture: base checkpoint, no LoRA, and a rig
the reference implementation built from its own proposal with no hand edit.
Its heavy inputs (the painting, the two images See-through was fed, and the
`full` and `head` See-through layer sets) live in the
[spine-parts-examples](https://github.com/firejune/spine-parts-examples)
repository, which also holds the generation record and the licence;
`bun run fetch-examples` copies them into `inputs/` here (gitignored). What is
tracked is the light half: `config.json` (the reference's config converted to
this schema, `src/config.ts`), `proposal.json` (what the proposer wrote — the
`propose` stage), and under `expected/` the reference implementation's outputs,
for the TypeScript stages to be compared against — `parts.json` (`assemble`;
its `visible_px`, `occluded_px` and `visible_not_projected_px`, and its
top-level `recomposite` block of uncovered holes, are this port's counts, which
the reference does not write, inserted from this port's own default build with
every reference value left as it was),
`rig.json` (the reference's, whose bones are all unturned; this port turns each chain link along its chain — issue #73 — so the built rig is compared through `flattenRig`, which turns every offset back, and matches it exactly), `motion.json` and `mesh_report.json` (`rig`), and `check.json` and
`gate_spine-html.txt` (`check`, the last being rig-c's gate lines); `check.json` is
regenerated by this port's `build`, because the reference has no judgement lines
— issue #11 — and again when the turned chains moved a judgement figure or two in
the third place through the runtime's float32 posing — issue #73 — and so is `motion.json`, whose blink holds 0.084 s where the
reference's held 0.04 s, less than one 12 fps frame — issue #32; its four hold-end
and open-end key times are the only values that differ from the reference's beyond the rounding of its float64 sums). Issue #119 (`assemble` clears a fringe below alpha 250 where the painting shows the part beneath it, which the reference did not) moved these from the reference's: `parts.json` holds this port's values for the 15 parts the rule touched — 276 px pushed back, each part's `fringe_pushed_back` list by holder, `opaque_px` and the three visibility counts taken after it, the boxes of `brow_r` (25 wide, was 27) and `iris_r` (19 wide, was 20), and `seam_override_px` one higher on `iris_r` — and this port's `recomposite` block (error px 4,359, was 4,512). `rig.json` holds this port's region offsets for `brow_r` and `iris_r` and this port's `hair_front` lattice (hull 56, was 54, with its uvs, weights and triangles), rebuilt on the 62 px the rule cleared from that part; `mesh_report.json` holds that mesh's hull and triangle count (200, was 202). `check.json` is this port's build of them (15 reported figures moved, every status as before). Issue #126 turned every mesh triangle counter-clockwise in Spine world, the winding rig-c reads a mesh in (the reference, and this port before it, wrote them clockwise there). The declared exception, and nothing else: every mesh `triangles` triple `[a, b, c]` is `[a, c, b]` in `rig.json`; the `check.json` `TEXTURE_STRETCH` entries that print a triangle's vertices and edge print them in that order (16 here, 52 over the three examples; the triangle, frame and figures are unchanged); and the untracked `skeleton.model.json`'s `spine.sha256`, which follows the skeleton bytes. Every rendered frame of the build is pixel-equal before and after. Issue #183 prepared this rig for a jiggle, and the config and these files carry it: two bones that are not the reference's, `chest_jiggle` (parent `chest`, at 419,320) and `hip_jiggle` (parent `hip`, at 419,520), each with a `physics` constraint (`bust_phys`, `hip_phys`) and no track of its own; `topwear` and `bottomwear` move from the lattice to contour mode (spacing their former grid, 18 and 28), because only a contour or automatic mesh carries a region, with a `circle` region on `chest_jiggle` (`bust`) and two on `hip_jiggle` (`hip_r`, `hip_l`). `rig.json` holds this port's bones, constraints and those two meshes (its other rows are as they were); `mesh_report.json` those two rows; `check.json` and `gate_spine-html.txt` are this port's build of it, whose loop line is read with the physics left out and whose `loop physics:` line names the two constraints. The idle keys `chest` and not `hip`, so in the idle the hip constraint moves nothing and the bust one moves its bone at most 0.066 rig px (idle frame 2): the constraints are there for a consumer's motion. `animations.json` is one such motion, and the public example of `motion.animations_from` (AUTHORING §3): a file in rig-c's motion-spec shape, named by the config, whose one animation, `hop` (1 s, not looped), keys only `hip`'s translate — a crouch to -5, a rise to +24 at 0.3 s, a landing at -6 at 0.45 s and a settle to 0 at 0.55 s — so `chest` follows through the hierarchy and nothing keys the jiggle bones. `build` writes it into `motion.json` after the idle, as read; `check` names it on its `beside the idle:` line and measures the idle alone. The two constraints are tuned for it (`bust_phys` inertia 0.25, strength 120, damping 0.92, mass 1; `hip_phys` inertia 0.2, strength 160, damping 0.88, mass 1.5): rendered at 30 fps, `hop` moves `chest_jiggle` at most 6.583 rig px and `hip_jiggle` at most 4.432 rig px from where keys alone put them (frame 13, the fall to the landing), and they overshoot after it (3.38 and 1.38 rig px at frames 21 and 22); check's texture-stretch reading over those frames is 1.697 (`topwear`) and 1.566 (`bottomwear`) against its ceiling 1.926544. `docs/evidence/sample-hop.png` shows frames 14 to 21. Adding `hop` widened the render's fitted viewport, which the idle frames share, and moved `check.json`'s `torso_heat_mean` from 7.8 to 7.705 with every status as before; the new constraint values moved `topwear`'s smallest idle stretch ratio from 0.979 to 0.98. `motion.json` holds `hop` after the idle, `rig.json` the two constraints' values, and `gate_spine-html.txt` counts two animations. `sheets/` holds the reference's
contact sheets of the two layer sets and of the assembled parts (`sheet`).
